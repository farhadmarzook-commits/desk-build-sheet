Before you buy any of this — the architecture that keeps you out of trouble

Patient data does not go on your own hardware. That isn't caution, it's what institutional policy says in writing at essentially every US academic medical centre — personal machines and personal drives are not approved systems of record for PHI, no matter how well encrypted. Encryption is a control; approval is a policy determination, and you can't self-certify it.

The standard pattern, and the one to build: identifiers live in your institution's REDCap — free to you through your CTSA or research informatics office, running on servers already approved for ePHI, with an audit trail, MFA and role-based access. You tag the identifier fields, and REDCap strips them automatically on export. The de-identified export is what comes to this desk, where R, your figures and your manuscript live. The linkage key never leaves REDCap.

Two things that catch trainees specifically: IRB approval has to come first — a chart review can only be determined exempt by the IRB, and retroactive approval doesn't exist, so data collected before approval generally can't be published. And don't build it in Excel — no audit trail, no validation, silent date coercion, no defensible provenance. REDCap in, R out.

So the hardware below is for the analysis side: securing the accounts that reach the real data, moving de-identified datasets safely, and keeping what's on your screen off other people's retinas.

The 32-inch display, settled

You asked whether ASUS is really the best. It isn't — and the honest reason is arithmetic, not brand preference.

macOS renders its interface at 2× and expects roughly 110 logical points per inch. A 32″ 6K panel lands at 112 pt/in — a dead-on match, and the reason 6K at this size is the correct target. A 32″ 8K panel lands at 140 pt/in, meaning UI and text about 25% smaller than Retina standard, so you'd run a fractional scaled mode and throw away most of the resolution you paid $8,799 for. The PA32KCX is a video-mastering reference monitor, not a desktop. Meanwhile a 40″ ultrawide and every 32″ BenQ sit at 140 PPI — the same density as the M80B you're replacing.

The Omni Pro is doing something real — a powered lumbar and an automated stretch cycle genuinely have no equivalent at Herman Miller, and if what you want is a chair that makes you move, nothing else does that. Still worth measuring

The desk is settled — these are the remaining unknowns. Send them whenever and I'll lock the layout.

1. Desk top thickness

The Ergotron LX Pro clamp takes 0.4″–2.4″. Grovemade goes to 3.2″; Humanscale stops at 2.0″.

If it's a problem — Drill a grommet and bolt through — more rigid anyway on a desk that moves.

2. Clear depth behind the rear edge

Standing-desk crossbars and controller boxes sit exactly where a clamp jaw needs to go. Most clamps need 2.5″–3″ unobstructed.

If it's a problem — Grommet mount, or shift the arm to a clear span.

3. Your correct seated desk height

Elbows ~90°, feet flat, thighs parallel to the floor. If this is below ~28.5″, the ALEX drawers are physically in the way.

If it's a problem — Move the ALEX out from under the desk.

4. Desk width, edge to edge

One 32″ panel is ~28″ wide. Adding the 27″ Apple alongside it needs roughly 52″ of clear span.

If it's a problem — Single display, or run the second one portrait.

5. Desk depth, front to back

A 32″ monitor wants ~30″ of viewing distance. The arm reclaims 6–8″ versus a monitor's own stand.

If it's a problem — The arm usually solves this by itself.

6. Your MacBook Pro's exact chip

Sets your display ceiling. M4/M5 Pro drive 2 externals, M5 Pro 3, Max chips 4. Apple menu › About This Mac.

If it's a problem — Everything on this list works on 2 displays.
